# Firm interview packs: practitioner sign-off

Checked Nov 2026. "Reported" = from public candidate reviews (mostly 2024–26), paraphrased; n = number of reports we read that mention it. Everything else is The Desk's view and needs a practitioner yes/no.

For each firm: tick **Approve**, or write the change.

## BlackRock

**Approve:** [ ] yes  [ ] change: ________

**What it tends to test (our view):**
- Genuine interest in markets and investing (have a view)
- Understanding clients: pensions, insurers, savers
- Active vs index investing, fees and risk
- Teamwork and how you learn fast

**Know before you go (firm facts, check):**
- The biggest asset manager in the world; manages money for institutions and individuals rather than lending.
- iShares is its ETF (index fund) business; Aladdin is its risk and portfolio technology, also sold to other firms.
- It has been growing in private markets and infrastructure.

**What recent candidates reported:**
- HireVue first: about 3 questions, roughly 2 minutes each with prep time
- Then several interviews, often framed around BlackRock’s principles, plus scenario questions
- Some roles add a task and a final round
- Why BlackRock (3)
- A recent global market or economic event and its impact (2)
- CV and past examples tied to BlackRock’s principles (1)
- Mostly behavioural, light technicals (2)

Sources: [Glassdoor, London analyst reviews 2024–26](https://www.glassdoor.ie/Interview/BlackRock-Interview-E9331-RVW91379954.htm) · [Glassdoor, further reviews](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/BlackRock-Interview-E9331-RVW98487490.htm)

**Why-us question:** Why BlackRock, and why asset management rather than investment banking? Be specific about the part of BlackRock you want to work in.

**Firm cases:**
- asset: A UK pension fund client is choosing between a low-cost index fund and an active equity fund with higher fees. How would you help them decide?
- risk: A client portfolio is heavily exposed to US technology shares. How would you describe the risk to the client, and what would you look at first?
- markets: Rates fall faster than expected. How might that affect a balanced portfolio of shares and bonds?

**Firm questions** (R = reported by candidates):
- BlackRock is known for iShares and for Aladdin. Pick one: how does it make money, and why does it matter to clients?
- Pitch me a stock, fund or asset class you would buy today. What is the case, what is the risk, and what would make you sell?
- What does it mean for an asset manager to act as a fiduciary for its clients? Give an example of where that would change a decision.
- Tell me about a time you had to learn something complicated quickly. How did you do it, and how did you check you had understood it?
- (R) Pick a recent global market event. What happened, and what does it mean for investors or businesses?

## Rothschild & Co

**Approve:** [ ] yes  [ ] change: ________

**What it tends to test (our view):**
- Why independent advice, not a bulge-bracket bank
- A deal you have followed, explained clearly
- Valuation basics and judgement
- Polish, discretion and attention to detail

**Know before you go (firm facts, check):**
- Family-controlled and independent: it advises, rather than lending its own balance sheet, so it pitches conflict-free advice.
- Main businesses: Global Advisory (M&A, debt advisory, restructuring), Wealth & Asset Management, and Five Arrows (its private markets arm).
- Strong in restructuring and debt advisory as well as M&A.

**What recent candidates reported:**
- One-to-one first round (analyst + associate): CV, motivation, technicals
- Assessment centre with commercial awareness and market news
- Some offices run a group investment-case discussion
- Why Rothschild / what sets it apart from other banks (2)
- Valuation: DCF, methods and their assumptions (2)
- Equity bridge (enterprise value to equity value) (1)
- Market news and commercial awareness (1)
- Restructuring team is more technical than M&A (1)

Sources: [Glassdoor, London analyst Apr 2025 + others](https://www.glassdoor.ie/Interview/Rothschild-and-Co-Interview-E9944-RVW96916386.htm) · [WallStreetOasis thread (older)](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/rothschild-first-round)

**Why-us question:** Why Rothschild & Co rather than a large bank with a balance sheet? What does independent advice give a client?

**Firm cases:**
- ibd: A family-owned UK manufacturer has an offer from a competitor and another from a private equity firm. As their adviser, how would you compare the two?
- deals: A company is struggling to repay its debt in 18 months. What options would you look at, and what would you need to know first?
- asset: A family wants to keep its wealth across generations. What would you ask before suggesting an investment approach?

**Firm questions** (R = reported by candidates):
- Tell me about an M&A deal you have followed recently. Why did the buyer do it, how was it paid for, and would you have advised it?
- When would a company need a restructuring adviser rather than an M&A adviser? What does that adviser actually do?
- Tell me about a time you produced work where the detail really mattered. How did you make sure it was right?
- (R) Walk me from enterprise value to equity value. What sits in the bridge, and why?
- (R) What sets Rothschild & Co apart from the other banks you are applying to?
