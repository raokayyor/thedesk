# Kayyor’s “They want to know…” copy — approval requested

Proposed 24 November 2026. Not published. These 50 are core journey and firm-pack priorities, not an analytics ranking. All are Desk practice questions unless an employer source explicitly confirms wording.

## 1. General

**Tell me about yourself and explain which parts of your experience are most relevant to Investment Banking / IBD at your target firm.**

They want to know which experience makes you relevant to this role. Give them your introduction, your strongest proof and your reason for applying.

## 2. General

**Tell me about an experience you are proud of. What did you personally do, what happened and how does it prepare you for Investment Banking / IBD?**

They want to know what you personally achieved and why it matters for this role. Explain your responsibility, your decision and the outcome you can support.

## 3. General

**A company is considering buying a competitor. How would you decide whether the price and funding make sense?**

They want to know how you would judge an acquisition. Separate the strategic case, the price and the funding, then explain what could make it a bad deal.

## 4. General

**Explain enterprise value versus equity value, and how debt and cash connect the two.**

They want to know whether you can separate the business value from the shareholders’ claim. Explain the debt and cash bridge and state your assumptions.

## 5. General

**Why your target firm and this Investment Banking / IBD role? Use a specific researched reason and evidence from your own experience.**

They want to know why you chose this firm over another. Connect one checked detail about its work to something you have done and your own reason for wanting it.

## 6. General

**What is 10,000 multiplied by 100,000? Talk me through how you would check it.**

They want to know whether your method is reliable. State the starting base and units, show the calculation, then check that the result makes sense.

## 7. General

**Estimate how many bicycles there are in Amsterdam. You do not need a known statistic: show your assumptions and how you reach a sensible estimate.**

They want to know how you reason when the answer is not obvious. State your assumptions, break the problem down and check the scale.

## 8. BlackRock

**Why BlackRock, and why asset management rather than investment banking? Be specific about the part of BlackRock you want to work in.**

They want to know which part of BlackRock, and why managing money rather than deals. “BlackRock” alone isn’t an answer.

## 9. Rothschild & Co

**Why Rothschild & Co rather than a large bank with a balance sheet? What does independent advice give a client?**

They want to know why independent advice, not a big bank with a balance sheet. Never say prestige. Say independence, and why it matters to a client.

## 10. Lazard

**Why Lazard? What would you get from a smaller independent adviser that you would not get at a larger bank?**

They want to know what you’d get at a smaller adviser that you wouldn’t at a big bank. Early responsibility, and sovereign advisory if it fits you.

## 11. Evercore

**Why Evercore and independent advisory rather than a bulge-bracket bank?**

They want to know why advice-only, not a bulge bracket. Don’t compare it to other advisers by name. Say what you want from independence.

## 12. J.P. Morgan

**Why J.P. Morgan, and why this team? Give a reason specific to J.P. Morgan rather than to any big bank.**

They want to know why J.P. Morgan, not just any big bank. Scale is the answer: it can lend as well as advise. Say why that matters for your team.

## 13. Goldman Sachs

**Why Goldman Sachs, and why this division specifically?**

They want to know why this division, not why Goldman. The division matters more than the logo, so spend most of the answer there.

## 14. Morgan Stanley

**Why Morgan Stanley? Use something specific about how the firm is built, not just its reputation.**

They want to know you understand how Morgan Stanley is built, not its reputation. Wealth management is a big part of it. Show you know the firm’s shape.

## 15. Barclays

**Why Barclays rather than a US bank? Give a reason linked to its UK and international business.**

They want to know why a UK bank with international reach, not a US bank. Answer that before they ask it.

## 16. HSBC

**Why HSBC? Link your answer to its international network.**

They want to know you understand the international network. Make it about a client crossing borders, not about the bank being big.

## 17. Citi

**Why Citi? Use its global network or services business in your answer.**

They want to know you understand what Citi does for multinationals. Few candidates mention services, so that alone makes you stand out.

## 18. Deutsche Bank

**Why Deutsche Bank? Use something about its business mix or clients.**

They want to know why this business and these clients matter to you. Pick one part of the bank, then connect it to work you have actually done.

## 19. Bank of America / Merrill

**Why Bank of America for this role?**

They want to know which part of Bank of America you want to work in, and why. Explain the work before you talk about the size of the bank.

## 20. UBS

**Why UBS? Link it to its wealth management focus.**

They want to know why a firm built around wealth management fits you. Explain the client problem you want to work on, then give your evidence.

## 21. EY

**Why EY, and why this service line? Give a reason specific to EY.**

They want to know what energises you, not only what you’ve done. Answer with a real moment, not a trait.

## 22. PwC

**Why PwC, and why this service line?**

They want to know what you want to do in this service line. Give a reason you have checked, then show how you have built a relationship or understood a business.

## 23. Deloitte

**Why Deloitte, and why this service line?**

They want to know which work brings out your strengths. Name the service line, then prove the link with one real example.

## 24. KPMG

**Why KPMG, and why this service line?**

They want to know why this work suits you and whether you will speak up when something is wrong. Give your reason and a real example, rather than listing values.

## 25. BlackRock

**BlackRock is known for iShares and for Aladdin. Pick one: how does it make money, and why does it matter to clients?**

They want to know whether you understand the business behind the name. Explain who pays for the product you chose and what problem it solves for that client.

## 26. BlackRock

**Pitch me a stock, fund or asset class you would buy today. What is the case, what is the risk, and what would make you sell?**

They want to know whether you can defend an investment view. Give the thesis, what the market may be missing, and the evidence that would make you change your mind.

## 27. BlackRock

**What does it mean for an asset manager to act as a fiduciary for its clients? Give an example of where that would change a decision.**

They want to know whether you put the client’s interests before your own. Give a decision where that duty changes the recommendation, not just a definition.

## 28. BlackRock

**Tell me about a time you had to learn something complicated quickly. How did you do it, and how did you check you had understood it?**

They want to know how you learn when you cannot already do the task. Explain your method and how you checked that your understanding was sound.

## 29. Rothschild & Co

**Tell me about an M&A deal you have followed recently. Why did the buyer do it, how was it paid for, and would you have advised it?**

They want to know whether you understand the buyer’s logic, rather than just the headline. Explain the funding, the risks and whether you agree with the decision.

## 30. Rothschild & Co

**When would a company need a restructuring adviser rather than an M&A adviser? What does that adviser actually do?**

They want to know whether you can distinguish financial distress from a sale opportunity. Explain the client’s problem and how the adviser’s work changes.

## 31. Rothschild & Co

**Tell me about a time you produced work where the detail really mattered. How did you make sure it was right?**

They want to know what you do to make your work reliable. Show your checking process and the consequence of getting that detail wrong.

## 32. Lazard

**Walk me through how you would value a company, and tell me which method you would trust most for a business that is not yet profitable.**

They want to know whether you can choose a valuation method for the business in front of you. Explain why profit-based methods may fail here and defend your alternative.

## 33. Lazard

**Tell me about a time you had to form a view with incomplete information.**

They want to know how you make a decision without pretending to know everything. Separate facts from assumptions and say how you tested the important uncertainty.

## 34. Evercore

**How do the three financial statements link? Walk me through what happens if depreciation goes up by 10.**

They want to know whether you can follow one change through all three statements. Explain the profit effect, the non-cash adjustment and the balance-sheet link, stating your tax assumption.

## 35. Evercore

**Tell me about a time you kept going on something difficult when it would have been easier to stop.**

They want to know how you respond when progress is difficult. Explain why you kept going, what you changed and what that effort achieved.

## 36. J.P. Morgan

**J.P. Morgan can advise a client and also lend to it. When is that an advantage for the client, and when could it be a conflict?**

They want to know whether you can see both sides of the bank’s business model. Explain the benefit of financing alongside advice, then the conflict and how it should be managed.

## 37. J.P. Morgan

**Tell me about something in the markets that has caught your attention in the last month, and why it matters for J.P. Morgan’s clients.**

They want to know whether you can connect a market event to a client decision. Explain the transmission mechanism and give your own view of what matters next.

## 38. J.P. Morgan

**Tell me about a time you worked in a team that disagreed. What did you do?**

They want to know how you helped the team resolve a disagreement. Explain the different views, your own intervention and the result.

## 39. Goldman Sachs

**Tell me about a time you failed. What happened, and what did you change afterwards?**

They want to know whether you can own a failure and change your approach. Be clear about your mistake, the consequence and what you now do differently.

## 40. Goldman Sachs

**Pick a company you follow. Is it a good investment today? Give the case for and against.**

They want to know whether you can argue an investment case in both directions. Distinguish a good business from a good price and say what would change your view.

## 41. Morgan Stanley

**Morgan Stanley has a very large wealth management business alongside investment banking. Why might that mix be valuable for the firm?**

They want to know whether you understand the firm’s business mix. Explain how wealth management and investment banking can complement each other, and acknowledge the risks.

## 42. Morgan Stanley

**Tell me about a time you did the right thing when it was not the easy option.**

They want to know whether your judgement holds when doing the right thing costs you something. Explain the choice you faced and why you made it.

## 43. Barclays

**Barclays’ values include integrity and stewardship. Tell me about a time you took responsibility for something that was not strictly your job.**

They want to know whether you take ownership beyond the task you were given. Show what you noticed, what you chose to do and who benefited.

## 44. Barclays

**How do higher UK interest rates affect a bank like Barclays: the good and the bad?**

They want to know whether you can explain both sides of a rate move for a bank. Connect income, funding costs and credit risk rather than assuming higher rates are always good.

## 45. HSBC

**A UK company wants to start selling into Asia. What could a bank like HSBC help it with?**

They want to know whether you start with the company’s needs. Explain the payments, funding and currency problems you would investigate before proposing services.

## 46. HSBC

**Tell me about a time you worked with people from very different backgrounds.**

They want to know how you adapt to people whose experience differs from yours. Explain what you learned, what you changed and how that helped the group.

## 47. Citi

**Why would a multinational company care which bank runs its payments and cash around the world?**

They want to know whether you understand the operational importance of transaction banking. Explain the consequences of unreliable payments or poor visibility of cash across countries.

## 48. Citi

**Tell me about a time you had to deliver for someone else to a tight deadline.**

They want to know whether you can deliver reliably under time pressure. Explain how you prioritised, communicated the trade-offs and checked the work.

## 49. Deutsche Bank

**How might a stronger euro affect a German exporter, and what could its bank help with?**

They want to know whether you can identify the exposure before proposing a hedge. State the currency, timing and downside, then explain the trade-off.

## 50. Bank of America / Merrill

**Tell me about a time you built a relationship with someone who was hard to work with.**

They want to know how you handle a different view without losing the relationship. Explain what you listened to, what you changed and what happened.
